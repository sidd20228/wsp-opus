## Design

**Strategy.** The player never moves left or right, so it stays in column 5, where it starts. Column 5 gives the longest warning from both edges: an enemy needs 4 or 5 moves to arrive. Every entity crosses every column, so all gold in a row eventually passes column 5. The policy therefore only chooses between staying, moving up and moving down.

**Danger timing.** Each row holds at most one entity, and the trail cell shows its direction. For an entity in row r, let t be the number of moves before it reaches column 5:
- t=0: the enemy is at column 5.
- t=1: the enemy is at column 4 moving right, or column 6 moving left.
- Entering or staying in a row with t ≤ 1 can kill the player this step, so those rows are "lethal".
- Rows with t=2 are bad because the player will have to leave next step.
- Rows whose enemy is moving away are safe.
- A row with gold cannot also hold an enemy, so it is perfectly safe.

**Layer 1 (32 units, 4 per row r = 1..8):**
- **P_r (units 0–7):** the player is in row r (output ±1).
- **L_r (units 8–15):** lethal threat in row r. It is a linear threshold on enemy and trail cells weighted by column. The values are about +1 for t=0, +0.83 for t=1, −0.83 for t=2, and −1 for anything farther, moving away or empty. Gold cells get a large negative weight so a gold trail cannot trigger it.
- **D_r (units 16–23):** a graded soft danger, high for t ≤ 1, medium for t=2, slight for t=3.
- **G_r (units 24–31):** an approaching treasure in row r, meaning at column 5 or moving toward it. It also covers treasure that has just spawned at column 0 or 9. Enemy cells cancel it.

**Layer 2 (gated by the player's row; inactive units sit at exactly −1, which the output biases cancel):**
- **U_y (units 0–6, y=2..8):** the advantage of moving up from row y. It is `tanh` of:
  - 12·(lethal_here − lethal_target) + 2·(soft_here − soft_target);
  - minus 1.5·soft danger two rows up, to account for a sticky repeat of the move;
  - plus a gold potential that pulls toward approaching gold, with weight decaying by distance;
  - minus 0.4 for moving into an edge row;
  - minus 0.5 move cost, so the player stays put when nothing is happening.
- **W_y (units 7–13, y=1..7):** the same for moving down.
- **X_y (units 14–21):** a tie-breaker between up and down that compares the danger of the two target rows and the rows beyond. It matters when both escapes saturate during a forced dodge.

**Output logits:**
- no-op = 0
- up = 15·U + 3·X
- down = 15·W − 3·X
- left, right and fire = −30

Moving into a lethal row therefore has a margin of about e^-15, idling is nearly deterministic, and the player dodges early at t=2.
